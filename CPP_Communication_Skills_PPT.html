HISTORY (55 seconds). Tell it as a problem-and-solution story. In 1972 Dennis Ritchie made C, a fast language. In 1979 Bjarne Stroustrup, working at Bell Labs, found C hard to organise for big projects. So he added "classes", a way of grouping related data and actions into neat units. In 1983 it was named C++. Explain the joke: in programming, "++" means "add one", so C++ means "C plus one step". Then 1998 first international standard, 2011 modern C++, and a new version about every three years. Delivery tip: name the year, pause, then say the event.

OBJECT-ORIENTED IDEAS (60 seconds). This is the heart of C++. A class is a blueprint, like the plan of a house. Objects are the actual houses built from that plan. Here, one Car blueprint produces three different cars. Then four big ideas, each with one picture. Encapsulation: you drive using the pedals, you never touch the engine. Inheritance: a Car is a kind of Vehicle, so it already knows how to move. Polymorphism: the same "speak" button makes a dog bark and a cat meow. Abstraction: a TV remote shows only what matters. Delivery tip: slow down here, this is the hardest part for non-technical listeners. Ask: "Does that make sense?" and look around.

CONTROL (55 seconds). "C++ hands you the steering wheel." Computer memory is like a row of numbered lockers; each locker holds one value. A pointer is just a note that says which locker to open. Other languages manage memory for you, which is safer but slower. C++ lets you manage it yourself: faster, but you carry the responsibility. Then one line about the STL: a ready-made toolbox of lists, dictionaries and sorting so you do not rebuild everything. Delivery tip: use the phrase "with great control comes great responsibility", then pause and smile.

WHY IT IS FAST (60 seconds). Three reasons, one picture each. One: translated in advance. Imagine a book translated into your language before you read it, versus someone translating each sentence live while you listen. C++ is translated once, in advance, into the language of the machine. Two: no middleman, it talks to the hardware directly. Three: you pay only for what you use, like packing a bag with only what the trip needs. On the right: the bar chart is illustrative, to give an idea of the difference, and real results depend on the task. Be honest about that; judges and teachers appreciate it. THE FAMILY (45 seconds). C is the parent, C++ is the hub. Many later languages borrowed ideas from C++. Java arrived in 1995 and borrowed its look and many ideas. C# came from Microsoft in 2000. D in 2001 is a cleaner redesign. Rust in 2015 aims for the same speed with safer memory. Carbon, announced in 2022 by Google, is an experimental successor. Careful wording: say "inspired by" or "borrowed ideas from", not "copied". Delivery tip: this is a good moment to look at the faculty and say, "So even if you never write C++, you have probably met its relatives."
